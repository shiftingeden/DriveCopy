Frequently asked questions

My drive doesn't show up.

iPhone can read drives formatted as exFAT, FAT32, or APFS — NTFS is not supported by iOS. If the drive draws a lot of power (spinning hard drives especially), connect it through a powered USB-C hub. When you tap an action, select the drive's root folder in the Files browser that opens.

Why does the app have to stay open during a copy?

iOS suspends apps in the background, which would pause the copy. Keep DriveCopy in the foreground for long copies — the progress screen says so, and the screen is kept awake for you.

Does Drive → Drive really need a hub?

Yes — the iPhone's single port can't power two drives at once. Any powered USB-C hub with two free ports works.

Is this a clone / bootable copy?

No. DriveCopy copies files and folders and verifies them with checksums. It does not copy partition tables, boot sectors, or hidden areas — iOS doesn't allow any app to do that. It's a backup tool for your files, and it's honest about that.

What's free, what's Pro?

The free tier covers copies up to 4 GB. DriveCopy Pro (one-time purchase, no subscription) removes the size limit and unlocks Drive → Drive. Restore a previous purchase from the paywall's Restore Purchases button while signed in with the same Apple Account.

Where do my backups live?

On your device, in DriveCopy's folder — also visible in the Files app under On My iPhone → DriveCopy, so your data is never locked in.
